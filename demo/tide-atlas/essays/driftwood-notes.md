# Driftwood Notes

*A short essay on objects that arrive without an address*

Driftwood is the shore's mail: everything the sea could not deliver, left at the tide line
with no return address. A good beachcombing day is a day when the sea has been sorting.

## What arrives

- **Pine, bleached white.** Travels far. The salt takes the colour out of it and leaves the grain.
- **Fishing floats.** Orange, then pink, then a colour with no name. The stamp on the side is
  always from a port you have never visited.
- **Rope.** Three-strand, usually nylon, usually in a knot someone tied with a purpose in mind.
- **The occasional message.** Not in a bottle — in a plastic crate, a shoe, a laminated card
  from a ferry that stopped running before we were born.

## What we keep

The rule at the field station: *photograph it, note where it was, leave it where it lies.*
Driftwood belongs to the next high tide, not to us. The archive keeps the photograph and the
coordinates; the object keeps its journey.

This is, more or less, how the whole Tide Atlas works. We are not collecting the coast. We are
keeping a careful record of having noticed it.
