# Reading a tide table

A tide table is written for people in a hurry, which is most people near water.

1. Times are local. Add the correction for your harbour, if you know it.
2. Heights are in metres above chart datum — **not** above the seabed, which is a different
   and much less reliable number.
3. "High 1" and "High 2" are the two daily highs. On this coast they are rarely equal; the
   second high is often the useful one, because the wind has had all day to push water in.
4. Ranges marked *spring* are the big ones. Neaps are quieter, and better for pools.
5. If the table and the water disagree, the water is right.

The tables in this archive are transcribed by hand from the harbour office board and cross-
checked against the keeper's notebook. Where the two disagree, both numbers are kept.
